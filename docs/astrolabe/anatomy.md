# Anatomy of the Astrolabe

An astrolabe is composed of several stacked components that rotate around a central pin.

## Mater (Mother)
The main body of the instrument. The front features a hollowed-out area (the womb) that holds the Tympans. The outer rim (the limb) is engraved with a 360° scale and often a 24-hour time scale. The back of the mater typically contains various measurement scales, such as a shadow square, calendar, and zodiac scale.

## Tympan (Plate or Climate)
A flat disc designed for a specific latitude. It is placed inside the Mater and remains stationary during use. It contains:
- **Tropic of Capricorn, Equator, and Tropic of Cancer**: Three concentric circles.
- **Horizon**: The boundary of the visible sky.
- **Almucantars**: Circles of constant altitude above the horizon.
- **Azimuths**: Arcs denoting compass directions from the Zenith to the Horizon.

## Rete (Network)
A rotating framework placed over the Tympan. It represents the celestial sphere. Because it is mostly cut away, you can see the Tympan's coordinate grid beneath it. It contains:
- **The Ecliptic**: An off-center circle representing the sun's apparent path through the zodiac over the year.
- **Star Pointers**: Small points extending from the framework, each marking the precise location of a bright star.

## Rule
A straight rotating arm attached to the front of the astrolabe, used to align the Rete and read time or angles off the limb of the Mater.

## Alidade
A straight rotating arm attached to the back of the astrolabe. It features two small sights (pinholes) and is used to physically measure the altitude of the sun or a star.
